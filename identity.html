03 / IDENTITY

Identity, without
blind trust.

Native decentralized identity records and an accountable verification marketplace form the identity layer proposed in Xenorra’s research.

IDENTITY RECORDS

Evidence behind every claim.

The protocol defines identity-related state and credential commitments, while keeping sensitive credential contents out of simple public-chain records.

01

Decentralized identifiers

DID documents can contain controllers, post-quantum verification methods and service endpoints.

02

Credential anchors

Salted commitments, schema identifiers, issuer DIDs and revocation indicators are recorded as native state.

03

Issuer and subject roles

Credential issuance and verification are modeled as distinct protocol activities.

04

Revocation and updates

Explicit state transitions support revocation and document updates subject to authorization rules.

ACCOUNTABLE VERIFICATION

Verification that can be challenged.

The research describes a verification market with structured request handling, bonded participants and dispute procedures.

01

Verification requests

A request defines work to be evaluated and the relevant verification parameters.

02

Commit–reveal

Verifiers first commit to results and later reveal them, reducing opportunities for strategic copying.

03

Aggregation and quorum

Verification results are aggregated under defined quorum and decision rules.

04

Challenges and collateral

Bonded dispute and challenge mechanisms aim to make dishonest verification economically accountable.

05

AI-assisted attestations

AI can assist in verification workflows; it does not eliminate the need for evidence, validation or accountability.

PRIVACY / RESEARCH BOUNDARY

Privacy by restraint, not by assumption.

Identity verification and transaction anonymity are different questions. Xenorra's draft keeps sensitive credential content outside simple public-chain records, but does not claim anonymous payments at launch.

01

Pseudonymous payments

Base-layer transfers use account identifiers rather than automatically revealing a person's civil identity. That is pseudonymity, not guaranteed anonymity: public transaction histories may still allow address and activity correlation.

02

Credential confidentiality

Salted credential commitments can anchor assertions without publishing the underlying personal data in the ledger. Privacy still depends on careful off-chain handling, disclosure practices, and threat modeling.

03

Future shielded research

The protocol draft explicitly defers payment privacy beyond pseudonymity to a shielded-pool research track. It does not specify a deployed shielded transfer system or promise untraceable transactions.

Research scope: a proposed design, not a deployed or audited privacy guarantee. Refer to the protocol specification for current assumptions and limitations.

Verification should be accountable, not unquestionable.
