01 / THE PREMISE

A different starting point.

Most blockchains were built for small classical signatures. Xenorra’s draft specification begins with post-quantum cryptography and redesigns consensus, history and identity around its consequences.

01

Post-quantum from genesis

ML-DSA-65 is specified for transactions, validator votes and node identity; SHA3-256 is used for consensus hashing.

02

The cost of authenticity

The paper describes 3,309-byte ML-DSA-65 signatures and 1,952-byte public keys. This changes the network’s bandwidth and storage economics.

03

A second recovery path

Hash-based SLH-DSA recovery is specified as an independent hedge against weakened lattice assumptions.

04

Research before rhetoric

The specification explicitly does not claim quantum-proof security or measured throughput. Independent review and benchmarking are still required.

THE RESEARCH THESIS

The cost of cryptographic permanence.

A blockchain cannot simply rotate away its history. The Xenorra research begins with a structural problem: post-quantum signatures are much larger than classical signatures, and a replicated ledger must account for their bandwidth, storage and verification costs.

01 / RESEARCH NOTE3,309

BYTES / ML-DSA-65 SIGNATURE

A standardized post-quantum transaction signature in the proposed cryptographic suite.

02 / RESEARCH NOTE1,952

BYTES / ML-DSA-65 PUBLIC KEY

The corresponding public-key size changes how authorization data is carried.

03 / RESEARCH NOTE02

SECONDS / DRAFT BLOCK TARGET

A design target in the research model, not a measured network result.

The protocol is designed around those constraints from the beginning, rather than treating quantum-resistant signatures as a later upgrade.

02 / NETWORK DESIGN

Precision beneath the surface.

A coherent architecture built from native execution modules, authenticated state and accountable consensus.

01

BFT proof of stake

Tendermint-family consensus, deterministic weighted proposer rotation and validator-signed commit certificates.

02

Signature witness separation

Authorization witnesses are segregated from transaction bodies and designed to be pruned after finality.

03

Identity as infrastructure

DID documents, salted credential commitments, issuer records and revocation are native protocol concerns.

04

Accountable Verification

A verification market with commit–reveal, aggregation, challenge procedures and bonded verifier incentives.

01 / AUTHENTICATEML-DSA-65

Transaction and validator authorization using post-quantum signatures.

02 / FINALIZEBFT consensus

Stake-weighted validator voting with deterministic proposer rotation.

03 / COMMITAuthenticated state

Sparse Merkle commitments anchor account and module state.

04 / RETAINPrunable witnesses

Separate authorization witnesses from long-term transaction history.
